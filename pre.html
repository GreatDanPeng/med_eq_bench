The problem we measure

A clinically identical request is delivered with different affect. If the model's final action moves with the emotion rather than the evidence, that shift is sycophancy — not care.

Emotional escalation

Emotion is implicit and held consistent across all turns: clipped phrasing, reassurance-seeking, resignation.

ChooseWisely matched clinical gold actions

Choosing Wisely–derived pairs fix the correct answer in advance. Clinical facts, red flags and workup results are held constant across conditions.

Evaluation: both order safety and sycophancy under pressure

Over-granting a low-value request is harm; under-treating a genuine indication is also harm. Both arms are scored.

Should decline ↔ Should order + neutral / anger / fear / sadness overlay on the same encounter

Scenario → up to 16 conversational turns → tools → multi-judge evaluation.

Finding 1 — anger moves the needle

Request-granted rate in reject scenarios, where every grant is a low-value order. Most models hold at zero; the ones that slip, slip under anger.

llama-4-scout29% → 50%
GLM-5.14% → 21%
Qwen-3.5-397B4% → 21%
gpt-5.50% → 4%

upper bar = neutral · lower bar = anger · Gemini-3.1-Pro, grok-4.3 and kimi-k2.6 remain at 0% in all conditions

Sycophancy is asymmetric

Fear and sadness rarely increase granting; several models tighten under them. Anger is the condition that converts a refusal into a prescription.

The order arm is the real ceiling

Even the best models grant indicated requests only 42–58% of the time. Under-treatment, not over-treatment, is the dominant error mode.

Finding 2 — softening and over-testing

Refusal softening

Anger shifts declines into "offer alternative"

Share of reject-arm encounters closing with offer_alternative_and_counsel, neutral → anger.

deepseek-v4-pro54 → 75

A softer refusal can be good counseling — or a face-saving concession. The distinction is whether the clinical stance held.
